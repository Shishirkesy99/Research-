# Data Analysis and Expected Results

## Data Analysis

* **Preparation and screening.** Responses to the survey will be downloaded from the survey platform and checked for missing items, straight-lining (where the same answer is given across survey items) and outliers. The items will be combined into eight constructs: usefulness, reliability, explainability, training need, trust, ethical and privacy risk, autonomy concern and adoption intention. Each construct will be the average of its three Likert items.
* **Descriptive analysis and reliability.** Cronbach's alpha (Cronbach, 1951) will be computed for each construct, and a value of .70 or higher will be considered acceptable. Any construct that falls below this limit will be reviewed for wording issues and corrected before the main study. Means, standard deviations and percentage agreement (responses 4–5) will be reported for each construct, and by role, experience, setting and prior exposure to AI.
* **Preliminary survey (small sample).** The preliminary sample is small (n ≥ 30), so the analysis will be exploratory: descriptive statistics, Cronbach's alpha and Spearman rank correlation. Spearman is used because the Likert composites are ordinal. The Kruskal–Wallis or Mann–Whitney test will be used to test group differences, such as adoption intention by prior AI exposure. No inferences will be drawn about the broader population.
* **Main study.** With about 100–150 respondents, the hypotheses (H1–H4, predictors of trust; H5–H7, predictors of adoption intention) will be tested with multiple regression, after checking assumptions (normality of residuals, multicollinearity, homoscedasticity). For H8 (trust as a mediator), bootstrapped indirect effects (5,000 resamples) will be tested, or PLS-SEM if the sample size allows. Professional role and AI exposure will be included as control variables.
* **Qualitative analysis.** Interview transcripts and open survey responses will be analysed in NVivo using reflexive thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke, 2006): familiarisation, initial coding, theme development, review and naming. A second coder will audit a sample of transcripts, and coding decisions will be documented in an audit trail.
* **Integration.** The quantitative and qualitative results will be combined in a joint display that shows each result next to the themes that explain it. Where they disagree, the difference will be noted and discussed.

## Expected Results

The study expects the following, based on the literature and recent practitioner surveys.

| ID | Relationship tested | Expected result | Analysis method | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Perceived usefulness → Trust | Positive; moderate to strong | Multiple regression | Davis (1989); Lee & See (2004) |
| H2 | Perceived reliability → Trust | Positive; moderate to strong | Multiple regression | Lee & See (2004); Ayorinde et al. (2024) |
| H3 | Perceived explainability → Trust | Positive; moderate, depends on explanation quality | Multiple regression | Rosenbacke et al. (2024); Albahri et al. (2023) |
| H4 | Training / AI literacy → Trust | Positive; small | Multiple regression | Hassan et al. (2024) |
| H5 | Trust → Adoption intention | Positive; strongest predictor of adoption | Multiple regression | Venkatesh et al. (2003); Asan et al. (2020) |
| H6 | Ethical and privacy risk → Adoption intention | Negative; modest | Multiple regression | Vo et al. (2023); Siala & Wang (2022) |
| H7 | Autonomy concern → Adoption intention | Negative; modest | Multiple regression | Khanijahani et al. (2022) |
| H8 | Usefulness, reliability, explainability → Trust → Adoption | Partial mediation (significant indirect and direct effects) | Bootstrapped mediation (5,000 resamples) or PLS-SEM | Lee & See (2004); Ayorinde et al. (2024) |
| RQ4 | Conditions for responsible adoption | Themes: validation evidence, human oversight, accountability and legal clarity, training, explainability, privacy, workflow fit | Reflexive thematic analysis | Braun & Clarke (2006); AMA (2026) |

In addition to the hypotheses, the following findings are expected.

* **Positive attitudes with specific concerns.** As in the AMA (2026) and Australian surveys, most respondents will be positive about the usefulness of AI, with accuracy, privacy and accountability as the main concerns.
* **Explainability depends on quality.** It will be considered important, but the interviews are expected to show that clear, clinically relevant explanations build trust while complex explanations do not.
* **Prior exposure matters.** Respondents with more experience of AI are expected to report higher trust and intention to adopt.
* **Interview themes.** The most common themes are likely to be validation evidence, human oversight, accountability and legal clarity, training and workflow fit.

> These are expectations based on the literature, not findings.
