2. Project Overview
•	Research Objectives:

The central goal of this research is to explore in depth the ethical aspects of the application of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning techniques in clinical decision-making, to ensure the safety, accountability and human-centricity of patient care. In doing so, the study investigates the risks of data privacy, security, and confidentiality in handling sensitive electronic health records and medical imaging data. This research aims to identify algorithmic biases and privacy risks, to offer practical evidence-based ethical recommendations to the Sydney Institute of Higher Education. This will provide a clear framework to steer the egalitarian, safe and transparent use of healthcare AI.

Research Questions/Hypotheses:

1.  Research Question: What are the primary ethical concerns, data security vulnerabilities, and privacy risks perceived by healthcare professionals and patients regarding AI-driven diagnostic tools? 
2.Research Question: How can AI models in healthcare maintain high clinical accuracy while ensuring transparency, safety of patients and the prevention of demographic biasness? 
Hypothesis: Transparent informed consent protocols that explicitly communicate how AI uses patient data significantly increase patient trust and willingness to opt into AI-assisted medical evaluations.





3. Methodology

•	Research Design:
This study uses a mixed-methods research design employing quantitative surveys and qualitative semi-structured interviews. The quantitative part measures the statistical trust, risk perception and acceptance of AI tools by healthcare workers and patients. The qualitative part collects in-depth views on clinical responsibility, algorithmic bias and ethical dilemmas in real world medical practice.
•	Participant Recruitment:
Voluntary convenience and purposive sampling methods will be used to recruit participants. Recruitment invitations will be sent out via official institutional communication channels, such as Moodle announcements, student and staff email broadcasts and digital notice boards. Your participation is voluntary and there will be no financial incentives and no pressure or academic penalties for participating or not participating.
•	Sample Size:

For the quantitative survey: I’m planning to gather responses from around 50 to 100 participants, including current health students, academic staff, and healthcare practitioners and other Individuals directly related to health care services.
            For the qualitative survey: I plan to speak with around 5 to 10 key stakeholders, such as clinicians, health                         IT professionals, gain deeper insights. These interviews will be semi structured and will continue until the ideas and themes reach a point of saturation.
•	Data Collection Methods:

 Online Questionnaire/Survey: Done and regulated via secure institutional Microsoft Forms. Contains closed ended scale questions and brief open-ended questions focused on AI perception and privacy. Estimated completion time will be around 10 to 15 minutes. 

    Semi-Structured Interviews: Conducted either face-to-face in a private room on campus or virtually via use of           Microsoft Teams. Estimated duration will be 20 to 30 minutes per session. Audio recordings will be captured with explicit participant consent and used for research purposes.

Data Analysis Plan:

Quantitative Data: Analysed using descriptive statistics like percentages, means, standard deviations and basic inferential statistics using Python identify trends, correlations. 
Qualitative Data: Audio recordings will be transcribed and will be analysed using Qualitative Thematic Analysis to identify recurring themes regarding medical accountability and privacy concerns.

4. Ethical Considerations
4.1.	Voluntary and Informed Participation

To ensure that participation is entirely voluntary and informed, participants will be provided with a plain English Participant Information Sheet detailing the purpose of the study, the procedures involved, the potential risks and how data will be treated. After this, informed consent will be formally documented (through a mandatory checkbox to agree before accessing the online survey or signed written and audio-recorded consent before individual interviews). Participants may withdraw at any time without needing to give reason or suffer any negative consequences. For those taking part in the survey, this can be done by simply closing their browser before submitting the survey, for those taking part in the interview they may request complete removal of their data up to two weeks after the interview.


4.2. Minimising Harm and Discomfort

The risk of this study is minimal. There are no physical procedures, invasive procedures, or sensitive personal topics that could possibly cause psychological, legal, cultural, or social harm. The only inconvenience to be expected is some minor fatigue when taking surveys or interviews. To deal with this, questions are limited to professional views, attitudes toward health care technology, and institutional policies, and exclude any personal medical histories or patient information. Participation is entirely voluntary, and people can choose not to answer questions or stop participating at any time. If you need support, the information sheet will also include contact details for SIHE Student Support Services.

4.3. Protecting Confidentiality and Privacy

 In order to protect the privacy of the participants, the research data will be stored on encrypted cloud storage that    is password-protected and with access limited only to the research team and the project supervisor. The quantitative survey responses will be obtained completely anonymously, so that no IP addresses or personal identifiers will be recorded. When the qualitative interview recordings and transcripts are processed, they will be de-identified right away by giving them alphanumeric codes such as Participant A and by eliminating any identifiable information about the institution. Files that connect real identities with pseudonyms will be kept separately in a secure, encrypted folder to ensure complete confidentiality.
4.4.	Ensuring Fair and Respectful Treatment

In order to ensure that participants are treated fairly and with respect, the recruitment of participants and the analysis of the data will follow inclusive and non-discriminatory standards about gender, age, ethnicity, and professional background. The various forms of communication used in the study, including the survey questions and interview questions, will be respectful, culturally sensitive and written in plain language. To avoid any researcher bias or unequal treatment, the data collection tools will include neutral and non-leading questions so that participants can give their genuine opinions without feeling pressured. Moreover, the findings will be assessed objectively when looking at the different groups of participants to make sure that all voices are represented accurately and without prejudice.
4.5. Honesty with Professional Colleagues

The research findings will be presented with complete honesty, accuracy, and transparency, making sure that there is no data fabrication, falsification, or selective reporting so that they truly represent the participants' insights. In order to preserve professional integrity, all contributions will be formally acknowledged: the project supervisor, as well as the academic colleagues, the group members, and the research participants will all be explicitly recognised in the final research documentation and in the GitHub submission records. Moreover, any limitations concerning the methodology or possible conflicts of interest will be openly stated in order to give a fair and well-rounded account of the study.
5. Data Management
•	Data Storage:
The raw research data, the anonymized survey responses, and the encrypted interview transcripts will be kept on password-protected institutional cloud storage (for example, OneDrive) and will also be backed up in a private GitHub repository. In accordance with the institution's research standards, the data collected will be securely maintained for at least five years after the project has been completed. Data Security: Access to the stored data will be limited to the main researcher and the project supervisor and will require multi-factor authentication (MFA) to prevent unauthorised access. The keys that link participant identities to pseudonyms will be kept in a separate, encrypted folder and will thus be stored apart from the main research data.

 
•	Data Disposal:

When the five-year retention period required has run its course, all the digital records will be permanently erased by means of secure file sanitization algorithms, and the printed notes or physical documents will be shredded using a cross-cut method.
